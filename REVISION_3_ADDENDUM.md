## 1. Provider options and capability tiers

Context: some people will want only on-device speech recognition; some (the owner) will use Claude; others will want Gemini, OpenAI, Grok, Microsoft Copilot or others. The owner wrote "Google Argon", which is unclear: do not guess; treat it as "Google's models (Gemini)" and flag it in QUESTIONS.md.

Design and document:
- **A provider-neutral model interface** (structure a note into records; answer a question from retrieved records via a bounded read-only tool loop; optionally describe an image, off by default), with **one adapter per provider**. Provider differences to cover: how structured output is enforced (JSON schema vs. tool use vs. other), tool-calling formats, token/price models, rate limits, error shapes, and data-retention/training terms. Include a **shared conformance test set** of sample notes (recorded responses for unit tests, plus a small live scoring run per provider) so filing quality can be compared and regressions caught. Say plainly that quality differs by model.
- **Bring-your-own-key and subscriptions.** Consumer chat subscriptions (Claude, ChatGPT, Copilot, SuperGrok, Google AI plans) generally do not include API access; each provider bills the API separately. Microsoft's consumer Copilot has no simple personal API as far as the owner's assistant knows; the usual route is Azure OpenAI. **Verify each from the provider's own current documentation, cite it, and state confidence.** Recommend which providers to support first (Claude is the owner's) and which to leave as "later/maybe".
- **Three capability tiers**, with a **feature-by-tier matrix** covering every feature in FEATURES.md:
  - **Tier 0, phone only:** on-device speech recognition plus code (rules, the personal word list, keyword/FTS search, templated answers). Say precisely which sentence shapes the rules handle, what degrades (messy sentences, splitting one note into several, readings from free speech, natural-language answers, trend questions, closing tasks by speech, photo label parsing), and how to make Tier 0 feel intentional rather than broken (visible "needs a model" hints, never silent failure). Notes captured without a model are **queued and filed later** if a model is turned on, which the raw-text-first design makes free: say how re-structuring of history works and what it costs.
  - **Tier 1, on-device model:** e.g. Gemini Nano via ML Kit GenAI/AICore or a small open model. Reuse Card 12's spike; state which phones, quality, download size, foreground-only limits, and whether it can do the extraction/answer jobs.
  - **Tier 2, hosted model with the user's own key** (any supported provider).
  Also per feature: what each **permission** unlocks and what is blocked without it (location, background location, notifications, microphone, photos/camera, nearby devices/Bluetooth, Wi-Fi state, activity recognition, exact alarms, and so on). Remember the owner's rule: during development he grants everything; tiers matter for the later product; build every capability behind its own switch now.
- **Explanation cards** in the app's settings (design them and write the wording for the main ones): for each choice the user makes, a plain card saying what it enables, what it sends and where, what is lost if it is off, and what it costs. Example shape: "Hosted model: better filing and answers. Sends note text (never audio, location or dates) to the provider you choose, under that provider's terms. Off: notes are filed by simple rules and some questions can't be answered." Add a "What leaves my phone" screen that lists, live, every kind of outbound request the app can make and whether it is currently on. Users are more comfortable when each change shows what it benefits and what it costs; that is the design goal.

## 2. Sensitive content (including adult images)

Screenshots, photos and screengrabs of adult or sexual material are a real case to handle by design, not by detection. Principles to adopt and document (in DESIGN.md, PRIVACY.md and as cards):
- **Do not build "explicit image detection" as a feature.** On-device classifiers are available but err both ways; a wrong label on a family photo is worse than none. Say so, and say what would change that view.
- **Every image is private by default:** stored in app-private storage (so it never appears in the phone's gallery or gets swept into automatic photo backup), encrypted at rest once encryption lands, never in lock-screen notifications, widgets, digests or recents (FLAG_SECURE on reading screens), never in diagnostic reports, never uploaded to a hosted model unless the user explicitly consents **per image**, and with a plain warning that providers' terms often forbid or refuse sexual content, so uploading could breach those terms or risk the user's account.
- **A "sensitive" mark** on any note or image (health, money, adult material, anything): sensitive items sit behind a second unlock, are never sent anywhere, and are excluded from digests, suggestions, search-as-you-type previews and diagnostics. The same mark **replaces the prototype's non-functional `localOnly` flag** (Revision 1 found that nothing ever sets it); unify them. Let the user say "this is private" by voice, and let the app suggest the mark for categories it recognises (boarding passes, medical readings) without ever forcing it.
- State plainly in PRIVACY.md and DESIGN.md that the app must contain nothing that helps share, upload or process sexual images of minors; that storing such material is illegal regardless of the app; that the design's protection is structural (images stay local, no sharing feature, no server of ours); and that if this ever becomes a product it needs a lawyer's view and a review of app-store policies on user content. This is not legal advice and should not be written as such.

## 3. Photo and screenshot flows (extends Revision 2 section 2)

Two directions, **one attachment model** (an attachment is linked to a note either way and either order; a note may be "photo pending" and stay complete if no photo arrives, with no nagging):
- **Photo first:** the user takes or shares a photo (Share to Stash from the screenshot preview/share sheet is the low-permission route; watching the media library is the higher-permission alternative, recommend). The app reads text on the image **on the phone** (on-device text recognition) to make a **draft**, then offers a voice annotation: "Anything to add about this?" Worked example: at Total Wine the user photographs a bottle. The wine's name, type, producer and any other label details should be searchable text in the note.
- **Note first:** the user says "It's a Valpolicella by Alegri, in the Italian section, row 5, bucket 10", and the app then asks quietly, "Want to take a picture of the bottle to add to this note?" The extraction step can return a **photo-suggested hint** when a note describes a physical thing (a bottle, a product, a part, a shelf location); Tier 0 can use a simpler rule for it. The offer is a button on the "Saved" screen or a notification action, vanishes on its own if ignored, is subject to the notification budget, and can be accepted by voice ("yes"). Check whether taking a photo from the lock screen works on Android 17/Samsung and say how sure you are.
- **Merging text sources:** label text (OCR draft) and the user's spoken words are both kept; if they disagree the user's words win and the label text stays on record. If the label can't be read and the user says nothing, the photo is still saved with time and place.
- **Data model additions:** attachments (photo, scan) with permanent IDs; image files stored at the minimum legible resolution with the format and compression chosen and justified; deletion with the note; a **"location within a place"** field ("row 5, bucket 10") as a normal field on a record; photo-pending state.
- **Searches to support, with no image recognition needed:** "what was that bottle at Total Wine a couple of weeks ago?" (place + date + text), "show me the bottles I tagged at Total Wine" (place + has-photo filter), and by wine name (stored text). Say how each is answered (router, tool, FTS) and add them as acceptance checks on the relevant cards.
- **Hosted vision** (sending the picture to a model to read it) stays **off by default, per-image consent, never for sensitive items**, and is a later option; say what it adds over on-device recognition and how it differs by provider.

## 4. Barcode and QR scanning

On-device scanning (e.g. ML Kit barcode scanning, or a camera library with an open decoder; verify licence, size, accuracy and permissions) as another attachment type:
- Store the **raw decoded payload, the symbology and a parsed type** (product number, URL, Wi-Fi config, payment, contact, ticket, other), attached to a note and/or a photo; also find codes inside photos already taken. Searchable by value. Re-scanning a known code can answer "you noted this one on <date> at <place>".
- **Safety:** a decoded QR/URL is shown as text and **never opened automatically**; opening needs a tap with the destination displayed first; Wi-Fi and payment payloads are never acted on by the app. State the risks (phishing, tracking links, Wi-Fi credentials).
- **Online product lookup** from a barcode (e.g. a public product database) reveals what the user was scanning: off by default, asks the first time, optional, and its outbound request appears on the "What leaves my phone" screen. Often the user's own note makes lookup unnecessary.
- **Personal codes** (boarding passes, tickets, loyalty cards, receipts) hold names and account numbers: recognise them and default to the "sensitive" mark.
