# Revision 1: from "where did I put it" to "capture anything, find it later"

You designed this project once already (see `README.md`, `docs/`, `docs/cards/`). That design is good but its scope is now too narrow, and a prototype exists and has been used on the owner's phone. **Revise the design; do not write app code.** A cheaper model (Sonnet) will build the cards.

Scope remains: the owner's own phone only (Samsung Galaxy S25 Edge, Android 17), no spend, no Play developer account (see `docs/DECISIONS.md` D34). Keep the design ready for other phones later, but do not plan for them now.

## What changed

The owner now says the app must be a **capture-anything inbox**. He will speak almost anything, submit, and move on with no follow-up and no verification, expecting the model to organise it for later retrieval. His examples:

- "I just tested the pool water, here are the results: free chlorine 5, pH 7.2, alkalinity 7, ..." (a set of measurements he will want to look up and compare over time)
- "I have to remember to clean out the back of the car soon." (a pending task he wants to be reminded of at intervals, by push notification ("You have things you wanted to do"), and by asking "What are the things I said I should handle?")
- the original examples: where he put something, who borrowed something, what to buy at Costco next time, with expiry on stale reminders.

This contradicts the current non-goal "not a to-do manager, journal or general assistant" in `docs/DESIGN.md`. Replace that non-goal with a scope statement that fits the new vision, and say what the app still deliberately is not.

The owner also asked, in his words: what is the correct format for storing the data; what agent type or other technology to use; what other features exist in this problem space that he hasn't thought of; and he needs a **credible story, for himself and for anyone he shows it to, for why local storage (or a better alternative) is the right place for personal information**, since "people get a little freaked out" about personal data leaking.

## Questions to answer, with reasons

1. **Data format.** Propose the storage model for a capture-anything inbox. Starting position to test, not a conclusion: keep the **raw transcript forever as the source of truth** and treat all model output as a re-derivable layer on top, so notes can be re-structured when models improve and a wrong guess never loses information. Decide: one utterance to many notes (splitting); a small fixed set of kinds (place or loan, task, reading, reference, idea, other) plus free-form tags and aliases; how **readings** are stored (name, value, unit, timestamp; trends over time; what "pool" or "car" means as a subject); the **task lifecycle** (open, done, snoozed, expired, dropped) and how "I cleaned out the car" closes a task; due hints ("soon", "this weekend", "before Friday") and how vague ones are turned into reminder schedules; versioning and migration from the prototype's current table (it holds real test notes; migration must not lose them).
2. **Agent type and technology.** Recommend the simplest thing that works. The starting position: **no autonomous agent**; one structured-extraction call at capture time (JSON schema or tool use) that can return several records; and, for questions, a **small bounded tool loop** where the model may call only the app's own read-only functions (search notes, list open tasks, get readings for a subject over time, look up a place), with a hard cap on steps and tokens. Reminders and scheduling are plain code, not the model. Evaluate: which model tier for each step; prompt and schema design; how to test it offline with recorded responses; how to measure extraction quality on real notes; cost per day; failure behaviour; what changes if an on-device model is used instead (Gemini Nano or a small open model on the phone: which phones, what quality, what download) and whether a "private notes never leave the phone, structured only by the on-device model or by rules" mode is realistic.
3. **Search and retrieval.** Full-text search with model-supplied aliases (already in the design), versus on-device embeddings, versus asking the model over retrieved notes. When each is warranted; how a question like "what was my pH last time?" or "what have I said I should handle?" is routed to a tool rather than free text search.
4. **Reminders and nudging.** A cadence that is helpful rather than annoying: per-task schedules, a daily or weekly digest, quiet hours, a notification budget, escalation and de-escalation (stop nagging after repeated ignores), the "What should I handle?" screen, and what a notification may reveal on the lock screen. Place-aware reminders stay in scope but are still deferred behind the basics.
5. **Features the owner has not thought of.** Research the problem space (personal knowledge capture, voice-first note apps, lifelogging, reminders and habit tools, "second brain" apps, assistants with memory) and propose features worth having, ranked must / should / later / won't with reasons. Prompts, not a limit: closing tasks by speaking; merging duplicates or "is this the same as something I already noted?"; trends and simple charts for readings; recurring readings (pool every week); staleness by kind (keys vs. a stored adapter); un-doing a mis-split; quick re-organise; search by date ("what did I say last Tuesday?"); a "needs attention" inbox for items the model was unsure about without making the owner verify each one; attachment of a photo; export in open formats; deleting everything; a read-aloud answer for when he is driving; how to handle a note that contains information about another person (a loan to Frank). Check each against what existing products already do and say what they got wrong.
6. **Privacy story.** Write `docs/PRIVACY.md`: a plain-language, honest account a non-expert could read and repeat. Cover: what is stored and where; what never leaves the phone; what does leave and when (note text to a hosted model only if enabled and the note isn't marked local-only), and what that provider does with it under its terms (**verify the current Anthropic API data-retention and training terms from Anthropic's own documentation, cite them, and say how confident you are**); why local storage over a cloud database (single point of breach and subpoena, account takeover, vendor lock-in, the owner controls it), with the honest costs of local (device loss, no sync, bugs in our encryption); encryption at rest and the known weakness while capture must write on a locked phone (already in DESIGN.md; restate it plainly); an alternative to local that is actually better if one exists (e.g. end-to-end encrypted sync to a folder or a home server he already runs); what the owner should and shouldn't put in notes in the prototype stage (the prototype database is unencrypted); and a short threat-model table. No marketing language; where it can't be promised, say so.
7. **Prototype to product path.** Say what in the prototype stays, what is replaced, and the shortest sequence of cards from today's prototype to something the owner can rely on daily on his own phone: encryption at rest and the app gate; the new data model and migration; the extraction call; tasks and reminders; readings and queries. Fewer, thicker steps than before are fine; the earlier cards were written for a four-phone family rollout and the owner's goal now is "does it feel useful to me".
