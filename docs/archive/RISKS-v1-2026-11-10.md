# Risks: assumptions that could invalidate the design, and how to test each cheaply

Each risk has a **spike**: a small experiment with a pass/fail result. Spikes are cards 01–06 in `docs/cards/`; results go in [EXPERIMENT_LOG.md](EXPERIMENT_LOG.md). Risks are ordered by how much of the design falls if the assumption is wrong.

Confidence marks are as in DESIGN.md: **high** = read in official documentation; **medium** = consistent with documentation or widely reported; **low** = inferred or conflicting.

## R1. A one-action voice note from a locked phone may not be possible on some of these phones

**Assumption:** on each phone at least one trigger can start a real microphone recording, with audible cues, without unlocking.

**Why it might be false:** Android only allows the microphone from a visible activity, a widget tap, a notification action, or the default-assistant service (high). Which hardware keys and gestures can reach those on a locked Samsung or Motorola is undocumented. Samsung's older help text says side-key "Open app" needs an unlock (low that double-press works locked). Android 17 silently drops sounds from the background, so a cue can fail without an error (high).

**What breaks:** must-have 1. Everything else still works, but the product loses its point if adding a note needs an unlock.

**Spike (card 01):** TriggerLab, a throwaway app with every candidate trigger, a 5-second record-and-play-back, and a checklist run on each phone, locked. **Pass:** on every phone, at least one trigger records real audio from the locked state with both cues heard (expected: notification action everywhere; assistant long-press on the owner's phone). **Fail on a phone:** that person's flow becomes "fingerprint, then widget"; tell the owner before building further.

**Cost:** half a day to build, an hour per phone.

## R2. Flip phones closed: the cover screens may not allow capture or answers

**Assumption:** the Flip 7 cover screen accepts our widget or notification action; the razr's external display runs the app.

**Why it might be false:** reviewers report the Flip 7 accepts only some third-party widgets and runs full apps only through Good Lock MultiStar (medium). Whether a third-party assistant appears on either cover screen is unknown (low).

**What breaks:** the parents (Flip 7) and the owner's wife (razr) would have to open the phone to add a note, which is exactly the friction the app is meant to remove.

**Spike (card 02):** add a cover-screen widget and a stand-in "ask" screen to TriggerLab; run the closed-phone checklist on both flips. **Pass:** closed capture on both; closed ask on at least the razr. **Partial:** capture only. **Fail:** neither on a phone.

**Cost:** two hours plus access to both phones.

## R3. On-device speech may not be accurate enough, especially for names and for the parents' voices

**Assumption:** a model small enough to bundle transcribes everyday sentences correctly and gets "Frank", "Costco" and "frunk" right most of the time, within about 3 seconds.

**Why it might be false:** small speech models are weakest on proper nouns and uncommon words; older voices and kitchen noise make it worse; published benchmarks disagree and none is for these phones (medium).

**What breaks:** must-have 7's "on-device by default". The mitigations (correction, "did you mean", read-back) carry a moderate error rate; they do not carry a high one. A cloud fallback is allowed by the brief only if accuracy requires it.

**Spike (card 03):** SpeechLab: record the brief's sentences and ten of the speaker's own on each phone, transcribe with each candidate (sherpa-onnx with Whisper/Moonshine/Parakeet models; Android's on-device recognizer), in aeroplane mode. **Pass:** 13 of 17 sentences right in meaning, names mostly right, under 3 seconds on the slowest phone. **Marginal:** names often wrong → proceed, rely on corrections. **Fail:** → larger model, or reopen the cloud question with the owner.

**Cost:** a day; the model downloads are the slow part.

## R4. The cheap model may not structure notes reliably, or the official SDK may not run on Android

**Assumption:** a Haiku-class model returns correct, schema-valid fields for at least 9 in 10 notes at around $0.0002 each, and Anthropic's Java SDK works in an Android release build.

**Why it might be false:** thinking tokens may inflate cost; the model may "helpfully" resolve dates or invent aliases; the SDK is built for servers and its Android behaviour is undocumented (low).

**What breaks:** little, by design: the rules structurer and coded answers stand without the model. But the long tail of phrasing would be handled worse, and cost or SDK trouble would change card 21.

**Spike (card 04):** 40 labelled sentences and 15 labelled questions run through the model on the JVM; token and cost measurement; one call from a minified Android build. **Pass:** ≥ 36/40 and ≥ 13/15, no invalid JSON, ≤ $0.10 per 100 notes, the Android call succeeds. **Fail on accuracy:** tune the prompt once, then consider the next model up. **Fail on SDK:** record why; fall back to a minimal hand-written client and tell the owner.

**Cost:** half a day and a few cents.

## R5. Arrival reminders may be unreliable because of battery management

**Assumption:** geofence broadcasts reach the app after days unopened, after a reboot, on One UI and on Motorola.

**Why it might be false:** Samsung puts unused apps to sleep (high in general; medium for its exact effect on geofences today). Geofences are dropped on reboot and must be re-registered (high).

**What breaks:** the "notify on arrival" half of must-have 4. The other half (show the list when the app is used at the place) needs none of this.

**Spike (card 05):** GeoLab: three places, one week per phone, default settings then "never sleeping"; compare notifications with real arrivals. **Pass:** ≥ 9/10 within 5 minutes with the recommended settings. **Fail:** ship opportunistic reminders only on that phone.

**Cost:** an hour to build, a week of normal life.

## R6. Updates may be too hard for the family phones

**Assumption:** the wife's and parents' phones can receive updates without the owner visiting.

**Why it might be false:** Samsung Auto Blocker blocks sideloaded installs by default (high); Google's developer verification will restrict sideloading from unregistered developers, first in a few countries from September 2026 and more widely from 2027 (medium; US timing not confirmed).

**What breaks:** not the design, but the plan to sideload. An app the parents cannot update will rot.

**Spike (card 06):** try sideload, in-app update prompt and (if the owner agrees) Play internal testing on the Flip 7 and the razr; count the steps a non-technical person must take. **Pass:** a route with zero or near-zero steps per update is chosen and written down.

**Cost:** two hours; $25 if Play is used.

## R7. The default-assistant route has side effects people will not accept, or does not stick

**Assumption:** a person can make the app their digital assistant without losing things they care about, and it stays that way.

**Why it might be false:** it replaces Gemini on the long-press and may disable "Hey Google" and features tied to the Google assistant (medium). One report says third-party assistants lose the role after updates (low). Samsung may route the key to Bixby.

**What breaks:** the best single trigger. The notification action, tile and widgets remain.

**Spike:** part of card 01 (row 4 and its notes): record what stopped working after the switch and whether the role survived a reinstall and a reboot. Then the owner answers Q1 per person.

## R8. The reading gate is enforced by code, not by cryptography (accepted for version 1)

**Assumption:** protecting against a person holding the phone is enough for now.

**Why it might be wrong:** the database key works while locked so that capture can save. Code running as the app on a locked phone could read notes. That is beyond a nosy relative or an ordinary thief, but it is a real gap between the stated policy and what is enforced.

**Test:** none needed; this is a known limit. **Mitigation:** card 30 (sealed inbox) closes it. The owner decides when (Q14).

## R9. People will not keep using it

**Assumption:** capture is easy enough, and answers trustworthy enough, that three different people form the habit.

**Why it might be false:** every memory aid dies this way. Google now ships "remember in Find Hub that I put my passport…" on these phones, which sets the convenience bar. Wrong answers early destroy trust.

**Test (not a spike card; a milestone):** after cards 01–20, the owner uses it alone for two weeks and logs: notes per day, questions per day, wrong or missing answers, and each time he did not bother. Then the try-with-a-parent session in card 27. **Pass:** the owner still uses it daily in week two and a parent can add and find a note unaided. **Fail:** fix capture friction before building anything else; consider whether Find Hub's feature is enough for the parents (Q11).
